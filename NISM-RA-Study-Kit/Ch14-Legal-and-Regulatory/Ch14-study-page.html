Legal and regulatory environment

Worth 10 marks of 100Book pages 255 to 294About 90 minutes

Who regulates the markets, which laws stop insider trading and manipulation, and the rules that keep a research analyst honest and independent.

14.1

The regulators

Regulators make sure market participants behave responsibly, so the market keeps financing companies and government, and investors are protected. Their shared aim: a fair and competitive marketplace with high service standards.

The five departments of the Ministry of Finance

Economic Affairs
Nodal agency for fiscal policy and the capital market including stock exchanges; external resources; bank notes and coins; prepares the Union Budget.
Expenditure
Government spending, financial rules, central employees' service conditions, help to and borrowing by states.
Revenue
Direct and indirect taxes, through the CBDT and CBEC.
Financial Services
Banks, insurance, pension reforms, industrial finance, MSMEs.
Disinvestment
Disinvestment and privatisation of public sector undertakings, and use of the proceeds.
Preambles worth knowingRBI: "to regulate the issue of Bank Notes and keeping of reserves with a view to securing monetary stability in India and generally to operate the currency and credit system of the country to its advantage." SEBI: "to protect the interests of investors in securities and to promote the development of, and to regulate the securities market."

SEBI's main functions

  • Protect investors; develop and regulate the securities market and stock exchanges
  • Register and regulate brokers, sub-brokers and other intermediaries; promote and regulate self-regulatory organisations
  • Investor education and training of intermediaries
  • Prohibit insider trading and fraudulent and unfair trade practices
  • Regulate substantial acquisition of shares and takeovers
  • Call for information, inspect, inquire and audit exchanges, intermediaries, SROs, mutual funds and others

For commodities, SEBI monitors forward markets, advises the government on recognising associations, and sets open position limits, circuit filters, margins and physical delivery rules.

14.2

The key laws

14.2.1 Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956

Gives SEBI direct and indirect control over almost all of the market: instruments, intermediaries, issuers and investors. It prevents undesirable transactions. It covers:

  • Recognising stock exchanges
  • Corporatisation and demutualisation of exchanges
  • Central Government power to call for periodic returns from exchanges
  • SEBI's power to make or amend exchanges' bye-laws
  • Central Government (also SEBI) power to supersede an exchange's governing body
  • Power to suspend an exchange's business
  • Power to prohibit undesirable speculation

14.2.2 SEBI Act, 1992

Section 11(1) makes it SEBI's duty to protect investors and to develop and regulate the market by measures it thinks fit. Section 11(2) lists those measures, including: regulating exchanges; registering and regulating brokers, sub-brokers, share transfer agents, bankers to an issue, trustees, registrars, merchant bankers, underwriters, portfolio managers, investment advisers, depositories, DPs, custodians, FIIs, credit rating agencies; registering venture capital funds and collective investment schemes including mutual funds; regulating SROs; prohibiting fraud, unfair practices and insider trading; regulating takeovers; inspection and audit; levying fees; and research. The Act also lets SEBI impose penalties and run adjudication.

14.2.3 Prohibition of Insider Trading (PIT) Regulations, 2015

Made under section 30 of the SEBI Act.

  • Regulation 3(1): an insider must not communicate or give access to UPSI to anyone, other insiders included, except for legitimate purposes, duties or legal obligations. Information is handled on a need-to-know basis.
  • Regulation 4: no insider may trade in listed (or to-be-listed) securities while in possession of UPSI. Such trades are presumed to be motivated by it; the insider may prove innocence in the circumstances the regulation allows.
  • Initial disclosure: a new key managerial person, director or promoter must disclose holdings to the company within seven days.
  • A compliance officer sets and monitors the codes of fair disclosure and conduct.
  • Fair disclosure: prompt, uniform, universal release of UPSI; no selective disclosure; information given to analysts must not be UPSI; record meetings with analysts.
  • Designated persons (including analysts, law firms, auditors, consultants) cannot trade while expected to hold UPSI; trades above board limits need pre-clearance.
The Chinese wallFirms separate "insider areas" that routinely see confidential information from "public areas" such as sales, marketing and investment advice. Insiders must not pass price-sensitive information across; teams may be physically separated. In exceptional cases a person may be brought "over the wall" on a need-to-know basis, and the compliance officer must be told.
Under the PIT section, the book quotes: analysts preparing reports on a client company must disclose their holdings to the compliance officer, and analysts who prepare a report on a listed company shall not trade in it for thirty days from preparation of the report. (The RA Regulations' own window, in 14.4, is different: 30 days before and 5 days after publication.)

14.2.4 Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices (PFUTP) Regulations, 2003

Also made under section 30 of the SEBI Act. Fraud (Regulation 2(1)(c)) includes any act, expression, omission or concealment to induce someone to deal in securities. Whether anyone gains or avoids a loss does not matter. Examples: wilful misrepresentation; false suggestion; active concealment; a promise made with no intention to keep it; reckless representation; false statements without reasonable grounds; an issuer's misinformation that moves the price.

Investigation: a person under investigation must produce books and documents and appear in person when required (Regulation 8(1)). SEBI may, pending or after investigation: suspend trading in a security; restrain persons from the market; suspend exchange or SRO office-bearers; impound proceeds; stop disposal of assets or securities. Against an intermediary: warning or censure, suspension, or cancellation of registration.

Mis-selling means selling securities or services by knowingly making false statements, concealing material facts or risks, or not taking reasonable care to ensure suitability for the buyer.

14.2.5 SEBI (Research Analyst) Regulations, 2014

Analysts' advice can be distorted by conflicts of interest. Regulators worldwide responded; the IOSCO report of September 2003 set principles: analysts' own trading and their firms' business must not prejudice research; reporting lines and pay should limit conflicts; firms need written procedures; undue influence of issuers and investors must be managed; disclosures must be complete, timely, clear and prominent; high integrity; investor education. India's RA Regulations followed.

14.2.6 Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

Consolidates insolvency laws for companies, partnership firms and individuals, with time-bound resolution.

Operational creditors can start insolvency proceedings but cannot sit on the committee of creditors. A financial creditor is one paid for the time value of money (earns interest).

Code of conduct for research analysts

Set out in the Third Schedule of the RA Regulations.

14.4

Conflicts of interest and disclosures

Chapter III of the RA Regulations.

  • Regulation 15: written internal policies on analysts' dealing and trading, to (i) address actual or potential conflicts from dealing in the subject company's securities, (ii) promote objective, reliable, unbiased research, (iii) prevent the use of research to manipulate the market; plus mechanisms to keep research independent of other business. 15A: RAs may charge fees as SEBI specifies.
  • Regulation 17 (compensation): no pay linked to a specific merchant banking, investment banking or brokerage deal; pay reviewed and approved annually by a board or committee with no representation from those divisions; analysts not supervised by those divisions.

More limits under regulation 16

  • Employed analysts' personal trading is monitored, recorded and, where needed, approved.
  • No trading contrary to one's own recommendation.
  • No buying or receiving shares of an issuer before its IPO if it is in the same business as companies the analyst covers.
  • These apply to a research entity too, unless research is segregated and at arm's length.

Regulation 18: other limits

  • Analysts may not take part in sales pitches or deal road shows for investment banking or brokerage business, or talk to clients about such deals in the presence of those divisions or company management.
  • Those divisions may not direct analysts to do sales or marketing.
  • Reports need adequate documentary basis; no promise of a favourable review in exchange for business; no report inconsistent with the analyst's own view; analysts kept separate from sales, trading and corporate finance staff (feedback from sales on a report's impact is allowed).

Other regulations in Chapter III onwards

19A Website
Maintain a functional website.
21 Public media
Disclose registration status and financial interest when appearing in public. Staff of IAs, CRAs, AMCs and fund managers commenting in media follow regs 16 and 17 and disclose name, registration status and interest.
22 Distribution
No selective early release to internal traders or favoured clients. Review third-party reports for false statements and disclose the third party's conflicts (unless there is no business relationship).
23 Proxy advisers
Covered by the RA Regulations. Review voting policies at least annually; alert clients of factual errors within 24 hours; communicate material revisions within 72 hours; share reports with clients and the company at the same time.
24 General
Arm's length between research and other activities; follow the code of conduct; SEBI's prior approval for a change in control; sole responsibility for client data and outputs when using AI tools; follow model portfolio guidelines and the Investor Charter. A model portfolio is a basket of securities in a research report that recommends weightages for them. A plain list of recommended stocks, with no weightages, is not a model portfolio.
25 Records
Signed, dated reports; recommendations and their rationale; public appearances; KYC; client register with PAN; communication records; client terms and consent. Kept at least five years. Annual compliance audit by a member of ICAI, ICSI or the Institute of Cost Accountants.
26 Compliance officer
A non-individual RA appoints a compliance officer, or an independent ICAI, ICSI or ICMAI professional with NISM certification.
26A Disputes
Mediation, conciliation and/or arbitration as SEBI specifies.
26B Grievances
Redress within 21 calendar days.
26C Segregation
An individual RA may not provide distribution services. Its family may not distribute to the RA's research clients. A non-individual RA keeps client-level segregation at group level: a client gets research or distribution, not both.
32 Default
Breaches, false information, missing returns, non-cooperation or unresolved complaints are dealt with under the SEBI Act or the Intermediaries Regulations, 2008.
14.5

Exchange surveillance: GSM and ASM

Some traders use unscrupulous practices that distort prices. SEBI and the exchanges watch stocks against objective criteria and impose extra restrictions.

The advertisement code for RAs and IAs

"Advertisement" covers all communications that may influence investment decisions, in any medium, print, electronic, social media or audio-visual. A research report counts as an advertisement if it promotes the RA's products or services.

Must include
  • Registered name, address, SEBI registration number, logo, CIN
  • Accurate, true, complete, concise information
  • The standard warning, unchanged, in at least 10-point font: "Investment in securities market is subject to market risks. Read all the related documents carefully before investing."
  • In audio-visual ads, the warning shown and spoken clearly (the book's example: 20 words over at least 10 seconds)
  • Translated accurately if the ad is not in English
  • For SMS or pop-ups without full details: a link to the website that has them
  • "The securities quoted are for illustration only and are not recommendatory" if securities are shown
  • The disclaimer that SEBI registration, BASL membership (for IAs) and NISM certification do not guarantee performance or returns
Must not include
  • False, misleading, biased or deceptive statements; misleading testimonials
  • Statements that exploit inexperience; exaggeration; excessive jargon
  • "Free" unless truly free
  • Any promise of assured, minimum or target returns, or percentage accuracy
  • Discrediting other intermediaries
  • Past performance of the RA
  • Superlatives: "Best", "No. 1", "Leading" (factual awards from independent bodies are allowed)
  • The SEBI logo

Also: get prior approval from the SEBI-recognised supervisory body; no ads while suspended; no games, leagues or prize schemes; keep a copy of each ad for five years. RAs may use brand names and logos if the registered name, number and contact details are shown prominently everywhere.

14.6

Investor charter and complaints

SEBI's Investor Charter for Research Analysts (Annexure 1 of the book) puts in one place the services, investors' rights, do's and don'ts and grievance timelines. RAs must share it: on their website and app, at their office, during onboarding, and by email or letter. RAs must also publish monthly complaint data, including SCORES complaints, in SEBI's format (Annexure 2); those without a website email it to investors.

The monthly table splits complaints by source: directly from investors, SEBI (SCORES) and other sources. For each it shows: pending at the end of last month, received, resolved, total pending, pending for more than 3 months, and average resolution time in days (total days taken ÷ complaints resolved that month). Complaints caused by someone impersonating the RA are reported separately and may be left out of the totals. RAs also show a month-by-month and a year-by-year trend of complaints received and resolved.

14.7 Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF)

All RAs must comply with SEBI's CSCRF. Its five cyber resiliency goals:

Anticipate

Stay prepared

Withstand

Keep running during an attack

Contain

Isolate trusted systems

Recover

Restore functions

Evolve

Adapt to reduce future harm

CSCRF grades regulated entities into five categories: Market Infrastructure Institutions, Qualified, Mid-size, Small-size and Self-certification REs. RAs not registered in any other category are excluded from submitting CSCRF compliance, but must submit a declaration under SEBI's 2020 SaaS (software-as-a-service) advisory. Institutional RAs registered in another category follow that category's classification.

All exam traps in this chapter

  1. SEBI was set up under section 3 of the SEBI Act, 1992; merged with the FMC on 28 September 2015.
  2. MoF's Department of Economic Affairs prepares the Union Budget; Department of Revenue works through CBDT and CBEC.
  3. MCA administers the Companies Act and the Competition Act 2002 (which replaced MRTP 1969).
  4. RBI administers FEMA 1999 and is banker to government and banks.
  5. PFRDA: PFRDA Act 2013, constituted October 2003; promotes old age income security; runs NPS.
  6. IBBI: under IBC 2016; also regulates valuers.
  7. SCRA 1956 gives SEBI power to make or amend exchange bye-laws.
  8. PIT 2015 and PFUTP 2003 are both made under section 30 of the SEBI Act.
  9. A connected person: associated with the company in the six months before the act.
  10. Change in ESG ratings is excluded from the UPSI list; other rating changes are included.
  11. Initial disclosure of holdings within seven days of appointment.
  12. Fraud under PFUTP does not need wrongful gain or loss avoidance.
  13. IOSCO principles on analyst conflicts: September 2003.
  14. RA qualification: graduate or CFA Charter plus NISM certification, or NISM's PG Program in Securities Market (Research Analysis).
  15. Form B for an RA, Form C for a part-time RA. Registration valid until suspended or cancelled.
  16. No trading 30 days before and 5 days after a report; never contrary to one's own recommendation.
  17. Quiet periods: 40 days after IPO pricing, 10 days after FPO pricing, 25 days for an IPO underwriter, 15 days either side of a lock-up end.
  18. Disclose ownership of 1% or more at the end of the month before publication; compensation over the past 12 months.
  19. Price chart: daily closes for the rating period or three years, whichever is shorter.
  20. Records kept at least five years; grievances redressed within 21 calendar days.
  21. An individual RA cannot provide distribution services.
  22. GSM is valuation-based; ASM is price and volume based (top 25 clients). ASM margin 80%, then up to 100%.
  23. Advertisements: no past performance, no superlatives, no SEBI logo, no assured returns; standard warning in at least 10-point font.
  24. Investor Charter: RA redress within 21 days; then SCORES 2.0 or RAASB; then SMART ODR.
  25. CSCRF goals: anticipate, withstand, contain, recover, evolve.
