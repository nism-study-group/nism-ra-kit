## Multiple-choice questions

**Q1.1.** What is the main role of a research analyst?  
a) Helping clients take informed investment decisions  
b) Doing financial planning for clients  
c) Only analysing data  
d) Only collecting data  

**Q1.2.** As per the SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014, a research analyst is a person who provides research services:  
a) Only as a full-time employee of a stock broker  
b) For consideration, and this includes a part-time research analyst  
c) Only to institutional investors  
d) Free of cost to the public  

**Q1.3.** Reports of which type of analyst are generally NOT available in the public domain?  
a) Buy-side analyst  
b) Sell-side analyst  
c) Independent analyst  
d) Media analyst  

**Q1.4.** An analyst at a broking and investment banking firm publishes a report with a 'buy' call and a price target. This analyst is best described as:  
a) Proxy adviser  
b) Independent  
c) Buy-side  
d) Sell-side  

**Q1.5.** Independent research analysts typically earn from their research by:  
a) Managing client money  
b) Charging brokerage on trades  
c) Selling research on a subscription basis  
d) Underwriting IPOs  

**Q1.6.** Which source would an analyst use for data on global factors?  
a) Industry journals  
b) International Monetary Fund and World Bank  
c) Company annual reports  
d) Plant visits  

**Q1.7.** According to the book, Keynes believed governments could change economic performance by adjusting:  
a) Stock market regulations  
b) Interest rates and money supply only  
c) Tax rates and government spending  
d) Exchange rates and tariffs  

**Q1.8.** Which of the following is a QUANTITATIVE factor in company analysis?  
a) Capacity utilisation  
b) Quality of management  
c) Brand power  
d) Customer satisfaction  

**Q1.9.** Why should an analyst cross-verify the claims made by a company's management?  
a) Because management never shares financial data  
b) Because SEBI bans all meetings with management  
c) Because clients prefer negative reports  
d) Because management may exaggerate positives to influence market prices  

**Q1.10.** While using their network, the most relevant contact for an analyst inside a company is:  
a) Always the CFO  
b) Always the CEO  
c) The person who understands the heartbeat of the company, who may not be in top management  
d) The company secretary only  

**Q1.11.** An analyst holds shares of the company she is recommending. What should she do?  
a) Disclose it beforehand in her communication  
b) Nothing, as long as the report is accurate  
c) Disclose it only if the client asks  
d) Disclose it after the price moves  

**Q1.12.** Which of these is NOT listed as a principle for meeting company management?  
a) Clarity of questions  
b) Pre-meeting research  
c) Asking management for non-public information to gain an edge  
d) Independence and neutrality of view  

**Q1.13.** Analysts may use recording devices while interviewing management:  
a) Only if SEBI is present  
b) Freely, without informing anyone  
c) Never  
d) Only after taking due consent  

**Q1.14.** In what order does the chapter describe an analyst's areas of study?  
a) Company, economy, industry  
b) Economy, industry, company  
c) Industry, economy, company  
d) Company, industry, economy  

### Answers

- **Q1.1: a**. The analyst does both research and analysis to help clients decide. Financial planning is an investment adviser's job.
- **Q1.2: b**. The definition turns on 'for consideration' (for a fee) and expressly includes part-time analysts.
- **Q1.3: a**. Buy-side reports are for internal use by the fund's managers and top management.
- **Q1.4: d**. Sell-side analysts work for broking, banking and advisory firms and publish public reports with price targets.
- **Q1.5: c**. Independent analysts work for boutique firms and sell research by subscription.
- **Q1.6: b**. Global data comes from bodies like the IMF, ADB and World Bank.
- **Q1.7: c**. The chapter quotes Keynes on tax rates and government spending.
- **Q1.8: a**. Capacity utilisation is a number, so it is quantitative even though it is not a money figure.
- **Q1.9: d**. Management may paint a rosy picture to get positive reports and lift the share price.
- **Q1.10: c**. The book says the right person understands the heartbeat of the company and may not be top management.
- **Q1.11: a**. Any conflict of interest should be disclosed beforehand.
- **Q1.12: c**. The opposite is true. Analysts must tell management not to reveal non-public information.
- **Q1.13: d**. The book recommends recording only after taking consent.
- **Q1.14: b**. Top down: economy first, then industry, then the company.

## Case C1: Priya's first coverage report

Priya works at a firm that does broking and investment banking. She is asked to cover a mid-sized cement company and publish a report for the public. Before meeting the CFO she reads three years of annual reports. During the meeting, the CFO says demand will double next year and offers to share next quarter's unpublished sales numbers. Priya also owns 500 shares of the company.

**C1-1.** Priya is best described as which type of analyst?  
a) Proxy adviser  
b) Sell-side  
c) Buy-side  
d) Independent  

**C1-2.** What should Priya do about the unpublished sales numbers?  
a) Ask the CFO not to reveal information that is not in the public domain  
b) Share them only with top clients  
c) Use them but not mention the source  
d) Use them, as the CFO offered  

**C1-3.** How should Priya treat the claim that demand will double?  
a) Accept it, as the CFO is senior  
b) Report it as fact  
c) Ignore all management claims  
d) Cross-verify it with suppliers, dealers, competitors and customers  

**C1-4.** About her 500 shares, Priya should:  
a) Transfer them to a relative  
b) Disclose the holding beforehand in the report  
c) Sell them quietly after publishing  
d) Say nothing since the holding is small  

### Answers

- **C1-1: b**. She works at a broking and investment banking firm and publishes for the public.
- **C1-2: a**. Under independence and neutrality, analysts must ask management not to reveal non-public information.
- **C1-3: d**. Management may paint a rosy picture. Cross-verify using her network.
- **C1-4: b**. Conflicts of interest are disclosed beforehand.
