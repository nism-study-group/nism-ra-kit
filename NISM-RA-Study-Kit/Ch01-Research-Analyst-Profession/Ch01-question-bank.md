## Multiple-choice questions

**Q1.1.** What is the main role of a research analyst?  
a) Helping clients take informed investment decisions  
b) Only analysing data  
c) Only collecting data  
d) Doing financial planning for clients  

**Q1.2.** As per the SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014, a research analyst is a person who provides research services:  
a) Free of cost to the public  
b) Only to institutional investors  
c) For consideration, and this includes a part-time research analyst  
d) Only as a full-time employee of a stock broker  

**Q1.3.** Reports of which type of analyst are generally NOT available in the public domain?  
a) Buy-side analyst  
b) Media analyst  
c) Independent analyst  
d) Sell-side analyst  

**Q1.4.** An analyst at a broking and investment banking firm publishes a report with a 'buy' call and a price target. This analyst is best described as:  
a) Proxy adviser  
b) Sell-side  
c) Independent  
d) Buy-side  

**Q1.5.** Independent research analysts typically earn from their research by:  
a) Selling research on a subscription basis  
b) Charging brokerage on trades  
c) Underwriting IPOs  
d) Managing client money  

**Q1.6.** Which source would an analyst use for data on global factors?  
a) Company annual reports  
b) Industry journals  
c) Plant visits  
d) International Monetary Fund and World Bank  

**Q1.7.** According to the book, Keynes believed governments could change economic performance by adjusting:  
a) Stock market regulations  
b) Interest rates and money supply only  
c) Exchange rates and tariffs  
d) Tax rates and government spending  

**Q1.8.** Which of the following is a QUANTITATIVE factor in company analysis?  
a) Brand power  
b) Quality of management  
c) Customer satisfaction  
d) Capacity utilisation  

**Q1.9.** Why should an analyst cross-verify the claims made by a company's management?  
a) Because management never shares financial data  
b) Because management may exaggerate positives to influence market prices  
c) Because SEBI bans all meetings with management  
d) Because clients prefer negative reports  

**Q1.10.** While using their network, the most relevant contact for an analyst inside a company is:  
a) The company secretary only  
b) The person who understands the heartbeat of the company, who may not be in top management  
c) Always the CFO  
d) Always the CEO  

**Q1.11.** An analyst holds shares of the company she is recommending. What should she do?  
a) Disclose it after the price moves  
b) Disclose it beforehand in her communication  
c) Disclose it only if the client asks  
d) Nothing, as long as the report is accurate  

**Q1.12.** Which of these is NOT listed as a principle for meeting company management?  
a) Independence and neutrality of view  
b) Pre-meeting research  
c) Asking management for non-public information to gain an edge  
d) Clarity of questions  

**Q1.13.** Analysts may use recording devices while interviewing management:  
a) Freely, without informing anyone  
b) Never  
c) Only after taking due consent  
d) Only if SEBI is present  

**Q1.14.** In what order does the chapter describe an analyst's areas of study?  
a) Economy, industry, company  
b) Company, economy, industry  
c) Company, industry, economy  
d) Industry, economy, company  

### Answers

- **Q1.1: a**. The analyst does both research and analysis to help clients decide. Financial planning is an investment adviser's job.
- **Q1.2: c**. The definition turns on 'for consideration' (for a fee) and expressly includes part-time analysts.
- **Q1.3: a**. Buy-side reports are for internal use by the fund's managers and top management.
- **Q1.4: b**. Sell-side analysts work for broking, banking and advisory firms and publish public reports with price targets.
- **Q1.5: a**. Independent analysts work for boutique firms and sell research by subscription.
- **Q1.6: d**. Global data comes from bodies like the IMF, ADB and World Bank.
- **Q1.7: d**. The chapter quotes Keynes on tax rates and government spending.
- **Q1.8: d**. Capacity utilisation is a number, so it is quantitative even though it is not a money figure.
- **Q1.9: b**. Management may paint a rosy picture to get positive reports and lift the share price.
- **Q1.10: b**. The book says the right person understands the heartbeat of the company and may not be top management.
- **Q1.11: b**. Any conflict of interest should be disclosed beforehand.
- **Q1.12: c**. The opposite is true. Analysts must tell management not to reveal non-public information.
- **Q1.13: c**. The book recommends recording only after taking consent.
- **Q1.14: a**. Top down: economy first, then industry, then the company.

## Case C1: Priya's first coverage report

Priya works at a firm that does broking and investment banking. She is asked to cover a mid-sized cement company and publish a report for the public. Before meeting the CFO she reads three years of annual reports. During the meeting, the CFO says demand will double next year and offers to share next quarter's unpublished sales numbers. Priya also owns 500 shares of the company.

**C1-1.** Priya is best described as which type of analyst?  
a) Proxy adviser  
b) Sell-side  
c) Buy-side  
d) Independent  

**C1-2.** What should Priya do about the unpublished sales numbers?  
a) Use them, as the CFO offered  
b) Use them but not mention the source  
c) Share them only with top clients  
d) Ask the CFO not to reveal information that is not in the public domain  

**C1-3.** How should Priya treat the claim that demand will double?  
a) Cross-verify it with suppliers, dealers, competitors and customers  
b) Report it as fact  
c) Accept it, as the CFO is senior  
d) Ignore all management claims  

**C1-4.** About her 500 shares, Priya should:  
a) Say nothing since the holding is small  
b) Transfer them to a relative  
c) Disclose the holding beforehand in the report  
d) Sell them quietly after publishing  

### Answers

- **C1-1: b**. She works at a broking and investment banking firm and publishes for the public.
- **C1-2: d**. Under independence and neutrality, analysts must ask management not to reveal non-public information.
- **C1-3: a**. Management may paint a rosy picture. Cross-verify using her network.
- **C1-4: c**. Conflicts of interest are disclosed beforehand.
