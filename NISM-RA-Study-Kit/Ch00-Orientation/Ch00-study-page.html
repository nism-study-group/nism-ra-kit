1

Why take this exam?

The exam is run by NISM, the National Institute of Securities Markets. Under a SEBI regulation of 2007 on certifying market professionals, NISM sets a minimum knowledge level for people who work in the securities markets.

This exam is Series XV: Research Analyst. It is required for people who prepare or publish research reports:

  • Research analysts registered with SEBI and their associated persons
  • People employed as research analysts
  • Partners of a research analyst firm
The legal hookRegulation 7(2) of the SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014 says associated persons must pass this exam. That is why it exists. You will study these regulations in Chapter 14.
A driving licence test. It does not make you a great driver. It proves you know the rules and the basics, so others can trust you on the road.

What passing gives you

  • A certificate, valid for 3 years.
  • The certificate is issued only if your PAN is in your NISM registration details. Add it when you register.
  • Passing does not by itself register you with SEBI as a research analyst. Registration is a separate process.

What does the paper look like?

It is a computer-based test. There are two kinds of questions.

This pattern started on 20 January 2026, when the syllabus was revised. Technical analysis and commodities were added. Our workbook is the February 2026 version. It applies to exams taken on or after 30 March 2026.

Time

120 minutes for 100 questions is about 72 seconds a question. Case questions take longer, because you must read the story first.

A suggested time planThis is our suggestion, not an NISM rule. Do the 80 MCQs first, in about 70 minutes. Then the 5 cases, about 7 minutes each. Keep the last 15 minutes to return to questions you marked for review.
A cricket innings of 100 balls. Take the easy singles first. Do not spend five overs on one difficult bowler.

How is it marked?

Right

+1

Wrong

−0.25

Skipped

0

So 4 wrong answers wipe out 1 right answer. Your net score is: right answers minus a quarter of wrong answers.

Getting 60 questions right is not enough if you also got some wrong. 65 right and 20 wrong gives exactly 60. 62 right and 38 wrong gives only 52.5.

The rule we will useA blind guess is barely better than a skip. If you can cross out even one option, answer. Skip only when you have no idea at all.

What is on the syllabus?

The book has 15 chapters. They are not worth the same. The marks below are the official weights from the workbook.

Four chapters, 8, 10, 14 and 15, carry 49 marks together. Chapters 1 to 3 carry only 5. We still read in order, because the early chapters teach the words the later ones use.

Pages are not marks

Some chapters are long but carry few marks. Others are short and carry many. The chart shows marks per 10 pages of the book. A longer bar means each page you read is worth more in the exam.

Chapter 2 is a long road with few toll booths. Chapter 13 is a short road with many. Do not judge a chapter's importance by its thickness.

What about numericals?

The test centre computers have either Microsoft Excel or LibreOffice Calc. You will not know which one in advance. The workbook advises being comfortable with both.

Numericals will come mostly from:

  • Chapter 8: financial ratios and DuPont analysis
  • Chapter 10: DCF, P/E, EV/EBITDA and other valuation measures
  • Chapter 12: returns, risk, beta, Sharpe and Treynor ratios
  • Chapter 3: bond yields, in a small way
Practise on a spreadsheetWhen we reach these chapters, do the practice questions in a spreadsheet, not on a calculator. Typing formulas quickly is part of the skill the exam tests.

How do I book the exam?

  • Register and book on NISM's certification portal: cert.nism.ac.in (linked from www.nism.ac.in).
  • Add your PAN to your profile, or the certificate will not be issued.
  • NISM publishes a list of test centres and dates every month. The October 2026 list has about 230 centres across India.
  • Most centres offer two afternoon slots: 13:30 to 15:30 and 15:30 to 17:30. A few offer other times. Each centre runs on certain dates only, so check your city in the latest month's list.
  • Helpdesk, as printed on the centre list: certification@nism.ac.in, phone +91 8080806476.
When to bookBook once you have finished a first pass of the book and taken a full mock test. Each centre runs only on some dates, so look at the list early.

We read the workbook one chapter per meeting, in order. That is 15 meetings after today. For each chapter there is one web page with three tabs.

Things people get wrong about this exam

  1. Thinking 60 right answers means a pass. Wrong answers pull the score down: 60 right and 8 wrong is only 58.
  2. Skipping every doubtful question. If you can rule out one option, guessing pays on average.
  3. Guessing wildly on questions you know nothing about. It barely helps and adds risk.
  4. Giving every chapter equal time. Chapters 8, 10, 14 and 15 are nearly half the paper.
  5. Judging a chapter by its length. Chapter 2 is 33 pages for 2 marks; Chapter 13 is 8 pages for 5 marks.
  6. Ignoring the 20 case questions. They are one fifth of the paper and take longer to read.
  7. Practising numericals only on a calculator. The centre gives you Excel or LibreOffice Calc.
  8. Studying from an older workbook. Use the February 2026 version; the pattern and syllabus changed in 2026.
  9. Forgetting to add your PAN. No PAN, no certificate.
