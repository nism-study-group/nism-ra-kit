### Answers

- **Q3.2: d**. Book sample question. EV = 10 + 3 &minus; 4 = 9 lakhs. Cash is subtracted.
- **Q3.3: d**. Share capital = number of shares &times; face value = 5 lakh &times; 10 = Rs 50 lakh.
- **Q3.4: a**. Dividend % is reckoned on face value: 25% &times; 5 = Rs 1.25.
- **Q3.5: d**. A split multiplies the shares and divides the face value by the same number.
- **Q3.6: a**. Consolidation merges shares, so face value rises. A split does the opposite.
- **Q3.7: b**. That is the book's definition of replacement value.
- **Q3.8: d**. Intrinsic value above price means the market is underpricing it: undervalued.
- **Q3.9: c**. The other options describe book value, market value and face value.
- **Q3.10: c**. The book says there is no specific cut-off and gives only a common rule of thumb. Small caps have low liquidity.
- **Q3.11: a**. The book names market cap to GDP.
- **Q3.12: a**. 500 + 200 + 50 &minus; 80 &minus; 20 = 650.
- **Q3.13: a**. The book allows balance sheet values when fair value cannot be determined.
- **Q3.14: d**. TTM is the popular form of trailing earnings. Forward earnings are projections.
- **Q3.15: d**. EBITDA is before depreciation and amortisation, so it includes the amount that recovers asset costs.
- **Q3.16: c**. EPS = 10 lakh &divide; 2 lakh = Rs 5. P/E = 60 &divide; 5 = 12.
- **Q3.17: b**. The book defines DPS &divide; EPS as the dividend payout ratio.
- **Q3.18: c**. Same price, lower forward P/E: next year's earnings are expected to be higher.
- **Q3.19: b**. P/S = market cap &divide; annual net sales = 400 &divide; 200 = 2.
- **Q3.20: d**. With losses, earnings-based multiples are meaningless. P/S still works.
- **Q3.21: a**. Banks carry mostly monetary assets valued near market prices. The book says P/BV may not be relevant for services firms with limited assets.
- **Q3.22: c**. The book warns that not all stocks trading below book value are bargains.
- **Q3.23: a**. DVRs carry fewer votes, not more.
- **Q3.24: b**. 25%. The 10% figure is the dividend record needed over the preceding 3 years.
- **Q3.25: c**. Yearly coupon = 1,000 &times; 7.5% = 75. Half-yearly = 37.50.
- **Q3.26: a**. The book says maturity is the single largest factor and the market risk in bonds is linked to it.
- **Q3.27: c**. The issuer is liable only for the principal, represented by face value.
- **Q3.28: c**. (16 + 1.6 + 10) &divide; 200 = 27.6 &divide; 200 = 13.8%.
- **Q3.29: d**. Current yield = coupon &divide; market price = 9 &divide; 90 = 10%.
- **Q3.30: b**. The book names ignoring future cash flows as the biggest drawback. Holding to maturity is an assumption of YTM.
- **Q3.31: b**. YTM equates present value of all cash flows to price: that is the IRR. Excel's XIRR can compute it.
- **Q3.32: b**. YTM assumes holding to maturity and reinvesting at the same rate, implying a flat and static yield curve.
- **Q3.33: b**. Modified duration = 5 &divide; 1.08 = 4.63.
- **Q3.34: c**. Duration rises with longer maturity, lower coupon and lower yield.
- **Q3.35: a**. The return on a zero is the gap between issue price and redemption value.
- **Q3.36: c**. Zeroes have higher duration, so more rate risk.
- **Q3.37: a**. All are under one year and issued at a discount. The money market is the segment for securities under one year.
- **Q3.38: b**. The book uses cap (maximum) and floor (minimum).
- **Q3.39: b**. The coupon resets upward with market rates, and price risk is low.
- **Q3.40: b**. Conversion issues new shares, so existing holders are diluted and EPS falls. That is the disadvantage.
- **Q3.41: d**. The book stresses that principal protection does not mean absence of credit risk.
- **Q3.42: c**. WPI for IIBs. The retail Inflation Indexed National Saving Securities 2013 use CPI.
- **Q3.43: a**. With foreign currency bonds, the issuer carries the currency risk.
- **Q3.44: b**. AT1 coupons are non-cumulative. A missed coupon is lost.
- **Q3.45: c**. Basis = spot &minus; futures.
- **Q3.46: d**. Futures above spot is contango. Futures below spot is backwardation.
- **Q3.47: d**. 1,00,000 + 1,00,000 &times; 6% &times; 6/12 = 1,03,000.
- **Q3.48: d**. The book lists storage, insurance, transportation, financing and other holding costs.

## Case C4: Sunrise Foods: pricing a share

Sunrise Foods Ltd has 50 lakh shares of face value Rs 10, trading at Rs 120. Last year's net profit was Rs 3 crore. Equity capital is Rs 5 crore and reserves are Rs 25 crore. It has debt of Rs 20 crore and cash of Rs 5 crore. The board declares a 25% dividend.

**C4-1.** What is Sunrise's P/E ratio?  
a) 12x  
b) 6x  
c) 20x  
d) 40x  

**C4-2.** What is Sunrise's P/BV ratio?  
a) 2x  
b) 0.5x  
c) 4x  
d) 24x  

**C4-3.** What is Sunrise's enterprise value?  
a) Rs 75 crore  
b) Rs 55 crore  
c) Rs 85 crore  
d) Rs 60 crore  

**C4-4.** What is the dividend payout ratio?  
a) 25%  
b) About 20.8%  
c) 2.5%  
d) About 41.7%  

### Answers

- **C4-1: c**. EPS = 3 crore &divide; 50 lakh = Rs 6. P/E = 120 &divide; 6 = 20.
- **C4-2: a**. Book value = (5 + 25) crore &divide; 50 lakh = Rs 60. P/BV = 120 &divide; 60 = 2. (4x would come from using only reserves.)
- **C4-3: a**. Market cap = 120 &times; 50 lakh = Rs 60 crore. EV = 60 + 20 &minus; 5 = Rs 75 crore.
- **C4-4: d**. DPS = 25% &times; Rs 10 = Rs 2.50. Payout = 2.50 &divide; 6 = 41.7%. 25% is the dividend rate on face value, not the payout.

## Case C5: Anita's bond and gold

Anita buys a bond with face value Rs 1,000 and a 9% coupon paid half-yearly, for Rs 1,050 in the market. She also watches gold: the spot price is Rs 1,00,000 for 10 grams, and the cost of carry is 6% a year.

**C5-1.** How much does Anita receive at each coupon payment?  
a) Rs 94.50  
b) Rs 90  
c) Rs 45  
d) Rs 47.25  

**C5-2.** What is the bond's current yield?  
a) 4.5%  
b) 9%  
c) About 9.45%  
d) About 8.57%  

**C5-3.** What is the fair value of a 6-month gold futures contract?  
a) Rs 1,00,000  
b) Rs 1,03,000  
c) Rs 97,000  
d) Rs 1,06,000  

**C5-4.** If the futures trades at that fair value, which is correct?  
a) The market is in backwardation and the basis is Rs 3,000  
b) The market is in contango and the basis is minus Rs 3,000  
c) The market is in backwardation and the basis is minus Rs 3,000  
d) The market is in contango and the basis is Rs 3,000  

### Answers

- **C5-1: c**. Coupon is on face value: 1,000 &times; 9% = 90 a year, so 45 every 6 months. The price she paid does not change it.
- **C5-2: d**. Current yield = yearly coupon &divide; market price = 90 &divide; 1,050 = 8.57%.
- **C5-3: b**. 1,00,000 + 1,00,000 &times; 6% &times; 6/12 = 1,03,000.
- **C5-4: b**. Futures above spot = contango. Basis = spot &minus; futures = 1,00,000 &minus; 1,03,000 = minus 3,000.
