## Case C20: Valuing Delta Foods

Delta Foods is a mature, steady dividend payer. The risk-free rate is 7%, the expected market return is 12% and Delta's beta is 0.8. Next year's dividend is expected to be Rs 4.40, growing at 6% a year forever. Delta's pre-tax cost of debt is 9%, its tax rate 25%, and its capital is 70% equity and 30% debt. It trades at a P/E of 22 with expected earnings growth of 20%.

**C20-1.** What is Delta's cost of equity under CAPM?  
a) 11%  
b) 15.8%  
c) 12%  
d) 9.6%  

**C20-2.** Using the Gordon growth model, what is the value of a Delta share?  
a) Rs 40  
b) Rs 93.28  
c) Rs 73.33  
d) Rs 88  

**C20-3.** What is Delta's WACC, closest to?  
a) 10.4%  
b) 8.3%  
c) 9.7%  
d) 11.0%  

**C20-4.** What is Delta's PEG ratio, and what does Lynch's rule of thumb suggest?  
a) 4.4; heavily overvalued  
b) 0.91; undervalued  
c) 1.1; clearly undervalued  
d) 1.1; slightly above 1, so not undervalued by the rule  

### Answers

- **C20-1: a**. 7 + 0.8 &times; (12 &minus; 7) = 11%.
- **C20-2: d**. 4.40 &divide; (0.11 &minus; 0.06) = 88.
- **C20-3: c**. 0.7 &times; 11 + 0.3 &times; 9 &times; 0.75 = 7.7 + 2.03 = 9.73%.
- **C20-4: d**. 22 &divide; 20 = 1.1. The rule treats a PEG below 1 as undervalued.
