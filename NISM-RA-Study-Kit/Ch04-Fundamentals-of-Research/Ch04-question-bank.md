### Answers

- **Q4.1: b**. Book sample question. Speculation is short term; investing is long term.
- **Q4.2: b**. Book sample question. Chartists use moving averages to reduce the effect of daily swings.
- **Q4.3: b**. Book sample question. Changing standards and business models make past data less comparable.
- **Q4.4: b**. That is the book's definition of a trader. The others describe investing.
- **Q4.5: d**. The book defines day traders by their one-day cycle. Chartists are defined by their use of charts.
- **Q4.6: b**. Or if risk falls without a proportionate fall in cash flow.
- **Q4.7: d**. Beating the asset class is the active investor's goal.
- **Q4.8: b**. Active strategies evaluate every security constantly, so they need more effort and trades.
- **Q4.9: a**. Research gathers information. Analysis processes it into a conclusion.
- **Q4.10: d**. The book lists facility visits and speaking to customers, suppliers and employees as primary research.
- **Q4.11: b**. The CEO's statement is material, non-public and price-sensitive. The employee's remark is not insider information, per the book.
- **Q4.12: c**. The book says mosaic analysis is acceptable.
- **Q4.13: d**. The book names source (reliability), impact and certainty.
- **Q4.14: a**. Technicians focus on price and volume because they believe the price already includes all information.
- **Q4.15: c**. The three elements are past prices, volume and time span.
- **Q4.16: a**. Support = buying interest. Resistance = selling interest.
- **Q4.17: d**. The book says a trend not supported by volumes may be weak.
- **Q4.18: a**. Fundamentals change over the long term, so past price trends become unreliable.
- **Q4.19: c**. EMH says prices already reflect all information. Fundamental analysis looks for prices that differ from fair value.
- **Q4.20: d**. Price above value is unattractive; price below value is a buying opportunity.
- **Q4.21: d**. Charts belong to technical analysis.
- **Q4.22: a**. Behavioural biases, driven by fear and greed, push prices away from fair value.
- **Q4.23: c**. Weather, input costs and production are supply factors.
- **Q4.24: b**. The book says higher rates strengthen the USD, lowering commodity prices.
- **Q4.25: a**. Jewellery 47%, investment 24%, central banks 23%, technology 6%.
- **Q4.26: c**. Gold is a safe haven. The other three are listed as negative for gold.
- **Q4.27: a**. Falling inventory means tighter supply. The others push crude down.
- **Q4.28: c**. WTI trades on NYMEX. Brent trades on ICE.
- **Q4.29: d**. Brent comes from the North Sea in the United Kingdom.
- **Q4.30: b**. The book defines OPEC+ as 13 OPEC members plus Russia.
- **Q4.31: a**. India is a top consumer of both. (China and USA are too, but are not among these options.)
- **Q4.32: a**. WTI is deliverable at Cushing, Oklahoma. With lockdowns, holders sold rather than take delivery.
- **Q4.33: c**. That is the book's account of the first oil crisis.
- **Q4.34: c**. Higher fuel costs hurt airlines and logistics firms; oil producers gain.

## Case C6: Priya's first week as an analyst

Priya joins an independent research firm. Her manager asks her to study a listed cement company. She reads its annual report, buys an industry report, and visits two dealers. At a family wedding, a cousin who is the company's finance head tells her the board will approve a merger next week. Separately, a truck driver at the plant tells her trucks have been queuing longer than usual.

**C6-1.** Visiting the dealers is an example of:  
a) Primary research  
b) Passive investing  
c) Technical analysis  
d) Secondary research  

**C6-2.** The cousin's news about the merger is best described as:  
a) Public information  
b) Technical signal  
c) Mosaic analysis  
d) Insider information that Priya must not use  

**C6-3.** Priya combines the truck queues with dealer feedback and public data to conclude demand is strong. This is:  
a) Insider trading  
b) Mosaic analysis, which is acceptable  
c) Behavioural bias  
d) Not allowed because the truck driver is not an official  

**C6-4.** Her manager wants a view on whether the share is worth buying for the next three years. Which approach is most suited?  
a) Day trading  
b) Technical analysis  
c) Fundamental analysis  
d) Pure chart reading  

### Answers

- **C6-1: a**. Speaking directly to people in the business is primary research. Buying the industry report is secondary research.
- **C6-2: d**. It is material, non-public, price-sensitive and from a reliable insider.
- **C6-3: b**. Small pieces, none significant alone, combined into an insight: mosaic analysis.
- **C6-4: c**. Fundamental analysis is focused on the long term. Technical analysis is less suited to long-term investing.

## Case C7: Oil shock at the morning meeting

At a morning meeting, analysts discuss the news: OPEC has announced production cuts, the US dollar has weakened, and US crude inventories have fallen. The stock market is falling on growth worries. The team covers an oil producer, an airline and a jewellery retailer.

**C7-1.** Based on the book's price drivers, the OPEC cut, weaker dollar and falling US inventory are all:  
a) Positive only for Brent, not WTI  
b) Negative for crude oil prices  
c) Positive for crude oil prices  
d) Neutral for crude oil  

**C7-2.** If crude prices rise, which company's profitability is most likely to fall?  
a) The airline  
b) Both equally  
c) Neither  
d) The oil producer  

**C7-3.** A falling stock market on growth worries is, as per the book:  
a) Negative for gold but positive for crude  
b) Positive for both  
c) Negative for both  
d) Positive for gold but negative for crude  

**C7-4.** Which statement about the weaker US dollar is correct?  
a) It is positive for crude only  
b) It is positive for both gold and crude  
c) It is positive for gold only  
d) It is negative for both  

### Answers

- **C7-1: c**. All three are listed as positive factors for crude.
- **C7-2: a**. Fuel is a major input cost for airlines.
- **C7-3: d**. Gold is a safe haven in bear markets. Crude does better in bull markets.
- **C7-4: b**. Both of the book's tables list a weaker dollar as positive.
