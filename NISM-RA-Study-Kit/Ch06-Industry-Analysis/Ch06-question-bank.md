## Case C10: Should Meena invest in airlines or education?

Meena compares two industries. Airlines: many carriers, similar service, fares matched daily, passengers switch easily, fuel supplied by a few oil companies. Private education: demand is growing, many licences are needed to open an institute, quality institutes are few, and students have little bargaining power.

**C10-1.** Which Porter force is clearly strong for airlines from the description?  
a) Threat of new entrants being low  
b) Industry rivalry  
c) Weak supplier power  
d) Weak buyer power  

**C10-2.** Many licences needed to open an institute are an example of:  
a) High barriers to entry  
b) High buyer power  
c) Low rivalry caused by price wars  
d) High threat of substitutes  

**C10-3.** Fuel supplied by a few oil companies suggests, for airlines:  
a) Strong supplier bargaining power  
b) High entry barriers  
c) Low threat of substitutes  
d) Weak supplier bargaining power  

**C10-4.** Which conclusion matches the book?  
a) Both are equally attractive  
b) Neither can be judged with Porter's model  
c) Aviation is attractive and education is unattractive  
d) Education is attractive and aviation is unattractive for shareholders  

### Answers

- **C10-1: b**. Many players, little differentiation, same pricing strategy and low switching cost: high rivalry.
- **C10-2: a**. Licensing is a classic entry barrier.
- **C10-3: a**. Few suppliers of a critical input means strong supplier power.
- **C10-4: d**. The book uses education as its attractive example and lists aviation as unattractive.

## Case C11: Profiling Speedline Telecom

Rahul is building an industry profile for Speedline Telecom, a mobile operator. Mobile prices fell sharply after a price war, and data use has surged, helping video apps. Speedline also owns a small, fast-growing fibre business with low market share, and a large, slow-growing landline business that still generates steady cash. Its taxable profit is Rs 50 crore.

**C11-1.** Which KPIs should Rahul track first for the mobile business?  
a) CASA ratio  
b) Same-store sales growth  
c) Passenger km  
d) ARPU and subscriber churn  

**C11-2.** Value moving from telecom companies to video apps is value migration:  
a) Across economic cycles  
b) Across industries due to regulation  
c) Across the value chain  
d) Across geographies  

**C11-3.** In the BCG matrix, the fibre business and the landline business are respectively a:  
a) Dog and star  
b) Question mark and cash cow  
c) Star and dog  
d) Cash cow and question mark  

**C11-4.** Which surcharge rate applies to Speedline under the book's figures?  
a) 7%  
b) 4%  
c) 12%  
d) Nil  

### Answers

- **C11-1: d**. The subscriber is the unit of pricing in telecom.
- **C11-2: c**. The book uses exactly this Indian telecom example.
- **C11-3: b**. Fast growth, low share = question mark. Slow growth, established share, steady cash = cash cow.
- **C11-4: c**. Income above Rs 10 crore attracts 12%. 4% is the cess.
