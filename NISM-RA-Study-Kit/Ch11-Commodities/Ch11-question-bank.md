### Answers

- **Q11.1: d**. Book sample question.
- **Q11.2: c**. Book sample question. The book names slowing growth, political instability, sanctions, currency volatility and trade policies. Rising growth and stability are the opposite.
- **Q11.3: c**. Book sample question. A weaker rupee amplifies the impact of global price rises.
- **Q11.4: a**. The other three are demand-side factors.
- **Q11.5: a**. An example of consumer preferences and lifestyle as a demand factor.
- **Q11.6: c**. The book also cites plant-based food affecting meat demand.
- **Q11.7: b**. Weather problems and labour strikes in Chile affect world copper supply.
- **Q11.8: c**. China and the USA are also in both lists, but are not among these options.
- **Q11.9: d**. The dollar became the preferred currency after the post-war period, followed by the euro.
- **Q11.10: c**. Euro, Japanese yen, pound sterling, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona and Swiss franc.
- **Q11.11: b**. The six are the euro, yen, pound, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona and Swiss franc.
- **Q11.12: c**. Commodities become dearer for non-dollar buyers, reducing demand.
- **Q11.13: b**. Dollar revenue converts into more home currency.
- **Q11.14: d**. CBOT: agri. COMEX: bullion. NYMEX: energy.
- **Q11.15: a**. Gold and silver.
- **Q11.16: d**. Global benchmarks set the tone for domestic markets.
- **Q11.17: b**. Strong local demand and seasonal supply can also cause divergence.
- **Q11.18: b**. Lower output projections can drive prices up.
- **Q11.19: a**. Adverse weather in Brazil disrupts coffee and soybean.
- **Q11.20: d**. Low inventory suggests scarcity.
- **Q11.21: a**. More supply, lower price.
- **Q11.22: a**. Low rates encourage borrowing and lift demand.
- **Q11.23: d**. Precious metals like gold act as an inflation hedge.
- **Q11.24: c**. Government policy directly shapes availability.
- **Q11.25: d**. The goal is stability of cash flows, not profit.
- **Q11.26: a**. If prices fall, the gain on short futures offsets the lower crop price.
- **Q11.27: c**. Long futures lock in the fuel cost.
- **Q11.28: b**. Over-hedging or wrong strategies can increase risk.
- **Q11.29: b**. The book's formula.
- **Q11.30: b**. 0.9 &times; 8 &divide; 10 = 0.72.
- **Q11.31: a**. 500 &times; 0.72 = 360 tonnes. 360 &divide; 2.5 = 144 contracts.
- **Q11.32: a**. Hedging also costs margins or premiums and needs expertise.
- **Q11.33: d**. Traders, importers, exporters and investors also hedge.

## Case C21: Kaveri Jewellers and the dollar

Kaveri Jewellers imports gold every month. This quarter, the US dollar index has risen sharply, the rupee has weakened against the dollar, and international gold prices are rising. India also reports a strong festive season.

**C21-1.** What does a rising dollar index usually do to international commodity prices, as per the book?  
a) Has no effect  
b) Fixes them at last year's level  
c) Puts downward pressure on them  
d) Pushes them sharply up  

**C21-2.** With international gold prices rising and the rupee weakening, Kaveri's rupee cost of gold will:  
a) Rise by less than the international price rise  
b) Stay the same  
c) Fall  
d) Rise by more than the international price rise  

**C21-3.** Strong festive demand in India is an example of:  
a) A geopolitical factor  
b) A supply-side factor  
c) Seasonality, a demand-side factor  
d) A currency factor  

**C21-4.** To protect against a further rise in gold prices, Kaveri should:  
a) Buy crude oil futures  
b) Sell gold futures  
c) Sell its gold stock  
d) Buy gold futures  

### Answers

- **C21-1: c**. A stronger dollar makes commodities dearer for non-dollar buyers, reducing demand.
- **C21-2: d**. A weakening domestic currency amplifies the impact of global price rises.
- **C21-3: c**. The book lists festive consumption spikes under seasonality.
- **C21-4: d**. Like the airline buying crude futures, a buyer of the commodity takes a long futures position.

## Case C22: Rao Cables hedges copper

Rao Cables needs 250 tonnes of copper every month. Its analyst finds a correlation of 0.95 between changes in spot and futures prices, a standard deviation of 9.0 for spot price changes and 10.0 for futures price changes. The MCX copper contract is 2.5 tonnes. LME prices have been rising.

**C22-1.** What is the hedge ratio?  
a) 0.855  
b) 1.056  
c) 0.900  
d) 0.950  

**C22-2.** How many tonnes should Rao hedge?  
a) 237.5 tonnes  
b) 213.75 tonnes  
c) 225 tonnes  
d) 250 tonnes  

**C22-3.** How many MCX contracts is that, rounded up?  
a) 214  
b) 86  
c) 85  
d) 100  

**C22-4.** Why do Indian copper prices tend to follow LME?  
a) Domestic base metal prices closely follow LME trends  
b) Copper is not traded in India  
c) SEBI fixes Indian prices to LME  
d) LME is an Indian exchange  

### Answers

- **C22-1: a**. 0.95 &times; 9.0 &divide; 10.0 = 0.855.
- **C22-2: b**. 250 &times; 0.855 = 213.75 tonnes.
- **C22-3: b**. 213.75 &divide; 2.5 = 85.5, so 86 contracts (the book rounds 36.6 up to 37 the same way).
- **C22-4: a**. LME is the global benchmark for base metals.
